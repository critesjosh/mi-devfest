# Notes from Marv K. (fictional), 31 years on the mills, talking through Op 20

Before I clamp anything I run my hand along both vise jaws and the parallels. Aluminum chips stick to everything, and one chip under the part will tip it. Then I tap the part down with a dead-blow and try to slide the parallels. If either one moves, the part isn't seated, so I unclamp and do it again. If it still rocks, I put a piece of shim stock under the low corner. The sheet says "clamp the part" and that's it.

Indicating the jaw, the sheet's got it right.

The big one is the first part. It comes off the machine warm, especially after a long roughing pass. Aluminum grows when it's warm. A 4 inch bracket that's maybe 40 degrees over shop temperature will read about two thousandths long. So I let it sit on the granite plate, or I hit it with coolant and wait ten minutes, before I mic it. I've watched new guys measure it hot, see it over, chase the wear offset, and then every part after that comes out short once it cools.

Also, I always check the pocket in two places, not one, because if the part tipped in the vise the pocket can be tapered.
